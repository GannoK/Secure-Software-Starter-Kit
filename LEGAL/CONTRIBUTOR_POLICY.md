# Contributor Policy

Status: active internal governance

## No automatic IP transfer or inbound public license

Opening an issue, receiving repository access, making a commit, submitting a pull request, delivering code/documentation, participating in chat, or receiving credit does not by itself transfer copyright ownership, create a public inbound license, or create a royalty/revenue-share obligation.

Substantive contributions intended for incorporation into project-owned IP require an applicable signed employment, contractor, contributor, or assignment agreement before final acceptance.

## Required representations

Before acceptance, a contributor must be able to represent that:
- they created the contribution or have authority to provide the necessary rights;
- the contribution does not knowingly infringe third-party rights;
- third-party and background material is disclosed;
- applicable licenses permit the intended use and redistribution;
- no undisclosed employer/client/confidentiality obligation conflicts;
- AI assistance is disclosed as required;
- secrets and private data are not included;
- promised compensation is documented separately.

## Security and supply-chain requirements

For code, dependencies, examples, or configuration:
- disclose dependency licenses and provenance;
- do not introduce unsupported or unmaintained dependencies without review;
- do not weaken established security controls without explicit evidence and decision;
- follow the project's security and verification workflows.

## Ownership mechanism

Preferred contributor agreements use:
1. work-made-for-hire treatment where legally available; and
2. a present fallback assignment of all assignable rights.

Do not rely on work-made-for-hire wording alone.

## Compensation

Unless a separate signed agreement expressly states otherwise, contribution does not create royalties, revenue share, profit participation, equity, ownership, approval rights, or continuing control over assigned work.

## Confidentiality

Repository access alone is not an NDA. Confidentiality must arise from a signed agreement, employment duty, applicable law, or another enforceable obligation.
